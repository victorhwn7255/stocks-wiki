# TDY — Teledyne Technologies Incorporated

## Thesis role

Teledyne is the Defense & Drones domain's **EO/IR sensor chokepoint owner** — Teledyne FLIR is the dominant Western position in cooled/uncooled thermal imagers, seekers, and gimbals, decades of export-controlled IP that the supply chain cannot quickly route around. Placement is **`defense_tier 4`** (supply-chain / payload enabler) per `frameworks-defense-drones.md` Framework D2 — the *highest-conviction* tier, where the chokepoint owners sit ([[MP]], [[LSCC]]). Teledyne carries no AI-datacenter supply-chain role (the AI `*_tier` + `layer` fields are not applied); a genuine-but-minor AI-adjacent slice is noted in prose, not tier-framed.

**Honest verdict (Q2 FY2026 refresh — two-sided; the strong side got stronger).** Teledyne delivered a **record quarter and a second consecutive beat-and-raise** — the defense/drone chokepoint thesis the vault prizes **strengthened**, and the broad-cycle commercial short-cycle **inflected positive**. Q2 FY2026: net sales **$1,662.5M (+9.8% YoY)**, GAAP operating income **$333.2M (+19.8%)**, **GAAP diluted EPS $5.37 (+21.2%)**, non-GAAP EPS **+20.8%**; **book-to-bill 1.23×** (Digital Imaging >1.4×), orders>sales for the **11th straight quarter**, **~$5B funded backlog**; FY2026 revenue raised again to **>$6.53B** and non-GAAP EPS to **$24.45–$24.65** (AEHR-style beat-and-raise cadence — the *second* raise this year) (Teledyne Q2 FY2026 call, Jul 22 2026; Q2 FY2026 10-Q). **But the honest counterweights all hold, and one new one appears.** First, **defense/drones is still only ~30–35% of revenue** (~$2B of a ~$6.5B company; the majority is broad-cycle commercial — semiconductor imaging, healthcare X-ray, MEMS, marine, T&M), so the EO/IR chokepoint is a high-quality *slice* of a diversified industrial-tech conglomerate. Second (**new this quarter**), **a ~$10M net tariff-refund** flattered the Digital Imaging margin by ~120bps — a trade-policy tailwind that could reverse. Third, management **still tempers** the surge narrative (won't chase 20% growth; margins "stay the same as projected"). Fourth, **Teledyne is itself squeezed from above** — its filings disclose China's restrictions on rare-earth minerals + permanent magnets impacting its products (it *owns* the EO/IR chokepoint downstream while *consuming* the rare-earth chokepoint upstream) — though that squeeze **did not bite Q2** (record results). Per Section 2.1, the page leads with the genuine chokepoint strength and surfaces the tensions plainly.

`_thesis.md` tracks: design wins, customer concentration, revenue visibility, differentiation vs broader semi test. The Defense & Drones disconfirming signal set lives in `_thesis-defense-drones.md`.

## Defense-budget exposure — benefit and hurt

Maps onto `frameworks-defense-drones.md` Framework D3 (demand map). Program facts primary (Teledyne Q2 FY2026 call, Jul 22 2026, unless noted).

**Benefit (reinforced at Q2).** Management sizes defense worldwide at **~30–35% of the company** (unchanged; "missiles and munitions… microwave and energetic components… make up the bulk of what is now about 30%–35% of the company's sales that go into defense worldwide" — Mehrabian). **2026 defense sales are guided to increase at high-single-digit rates with pockets of double-digit growth** (many Q1/Q2 bookings are multi-year). Demand signals strengthened: **book-to-bill 1.23× (Digital Imaging >1.4×), the 11th consecutive quarter orders exceeded sales; ~$5B funded backlog** (from ~$4.6B at Q1); infrared detectors for space-based imaging **+>20%**; SDA/space and unmanned both **+>10%**. **★ Missiles & munitions ~$200–250M revenue run-rate** (microwave + energetic components/subsystems; programs from AMRAAM → PAC-3, Hellfire → Javelin, plus new hypersonics work) — with Middle-East conflict driving customer inquiries for increased production and **US-government investment in manufacturing upgrades** (a >$30M Engineered Systems commitment named specifically; European customers like MBDA similar). Teledyne **raised FY2026 guidance a second time** on the momentum.

**Hurt (and management's own tempering).** The defense tailwind is real but bounded and lumpy, and — unlike [[KTOS]]'s promotional framing — **Teledyne management actively tempers the budget-surge narrative**:
- *Diversification ceiling:* defense is only ~30–35%; the broad-cycle commercial majority drives most of the company. (In Q2 that majority *inflected positive* — see the short-cycle note below — a benefit, but it underlines that the company is not a defense pure-play.)
- *Procurement timing / shutdown risk (Tier 1-disclosed):* the FY2025 10-K + Q2 FY2026 10-Q flag that "**U.S. Government shutdowns**… have resulted in **delays in anticipated contract awards, delayed payments of invoices** and delays in the issuance" of awards.
- *Surge skepticism (persistent):* Mehrabian repeatedly hedges — "we always have to be a little cautious to make sure the numbers… are numbers that we can meet"; on the full-year margin, "we're kind of being a little cautious… the margins will stay the same as we projected before." Per Section 3.3, this is Tier 2 management characterization — the *inverse* of [[KTOS]]'s lean-in; it cross-references the `_thesis-defense-drones.md` **enacted-vs-requested budget-contingency caveat**, with the twist that *Teledyne itself supplies the tempering*.
- *Enacted-vs-requested — a partial conversion datapoint:* the **>$30M government manufacturing-upgrade commitment** (Engineered Systems) is a concrete instance of requested budget converting to obligated Teledyne funding — a small but real conversion signal on the caveat.

**Short-cycle commercial inflection (Q2, the benefit beyond defense).** Management raised the *commercial* outlook: industrial inspection + healthcare, which had seen headwinds, "have indeed begun" to inflect; Teledyne is now comfortable with **mid-single-digit growth across the commercial portfolio** (from flat-to-low-single prior). DALSA/e2v industrial & scientific vision +>8%, healthcare X-ray detectors +>8.5%, test & measurement +4% (oscilloscopes/protocol analyzers) (Bobb).

## Drone supercycle — benefit and hurt

**Benefit (the right side of commoditization — reinforced).** Teledyne's drone exposure is multi-vector and weighted to the *high-value sensor/payload layer*:
- **★ Unmanned business quantified + growing:** **~$500M (2025) → ~$575M (2026e), ~12% growth** (air + ground + underwater; the 2025 split was ~$400M in Digital Imaging [primarily air] + ~$100M underwater) (Mehrabian). Both unmanned and space grew **>10%** in Q2.
- **Black Hornet 4** nano-UAS now selling ("very successful; we will probably introduce a whole series of new products in that domain"). The Black Hornet's cells come from the battery enabler [[AMPX]] (Amprius names Teledyne FLIR as a customer; SiCore cells designed into FLIR UAS programs) — a node where Teledyne *consumes* the battery chokepoint even as it *owns* the sensor one.
- **★ Picks-and-shovels sensor supply (the structural point):** Teledyne supplies "cooled, visible, and more importantly, **infrared detectors, not only to our own drone manufacturers, but also to everyone else across the world that's making drones**" (Mehrabian). Teledyne wins at the payload/seeker layer **regardless of which airframe wins** — the inverse exposure to the platform primes [[AVAV]] and [[KTOS]]. As the thesis predicts margin migrates upstream from commoditizing airframes (see [[drone-platform-commoditization]]), Teledyne sits at the layer the value migrates *to* — and Q2's Digital Imaging non-GAAP margin of **25% (+353bps)** is that value showing up.
- **Unmanned subsea** (Gavia mine-countermeasure vehicles, long-endurance gliders; UK/Europe strong orders) and **unmanned surface vessels** (Teledyne cameras on the USVs that saved two helicopter pilots and attacked a submarine in the Strait of Hormuz) round out the air/ground/sea franchise.

**Hurt.** Drones are a *slice of the ~$2B defense* (itself a slice of ~$6.5B). The acute risk is **the upstream squeeze** (next section), not airframe commoditization — the sensor layer is defensible against cheap airframes, but **ASP discipline on high-volume cheap counter-UAS sensors** is still a watch item management did not address (a Source-audit gap; OQ). **Rogue 1 / LASSO stays unnamed at Q2** — Mehrabian answered the loitering-munitions / MAC-framework question at the missiles-&-munitions run-rate level ($200–250M) without breaking out Rogue 1, so its program scope remains unquantified.

## The two-sided China exposure + the chokepoint-consumer finding (honest-verdict centerpiece)

Teledyne *owns* the EO/IR sensor chokepoint, but it is **squeezed from above by the same China concentration [[MP]] is reshoring** — and it says so in its own filings. The Q2 FY2026 10-Q retains the core disclosure (in slightly abbreviated, forward-conditional form):

> "China has also restricted the export of certain **rare earth minerals** that we use in our products, which **could disrupt** the supply chain for these minerals and components made from these materials." (Teledyne Q2 FY2026 10-Q; the FY2025 10-K framed it as "**has in the past delayed**" and added **germanium + gallium** specificity that the condensed Q2 10-Q drops.)

> "...new restrictions on the supply of **rare earth minerals and permanent magnets** from China…" (Q2 FY2026 10-Q risk factors.)

Two points fall out:
- **Rare-earth NdFeB magnets** → Teledyne remains the **second disclosed defense consumer of the magnet chokepoint** (after [[AVAV]]), naming rare-earth minerals + permanent magnets "used in our products." This is the pre-registered anchor that promoted [[rare-earth-magnet-chokepoint]] from provisional to canonical (S131).
- **Germanium / gallium (EO/IR optics + compound semiconductors)** → the FY2025 10-K's granular germanium/gallium language (germanium is the load-bearing IR-window material for thermal optics) is **not repeated in the condensed Q2 10-Q** — a filing-form abbreviation, not a resolution of the dependence; carry it as a 10-K-scope forward dimension.

**★ The Q2 finding — the squeeze did NOT bite, and China's *trade* dynamics were a net benefit this quarter.** Despite the disclosed rare-earth restriction, Q2 was a record quarter with margins up — no material supply constraint on Digital Imaging or A&D shipments. And on the trade side, a **~$10M net tariff-refund** (mostly Digital Imaging) contributed ~120bps of DI's 353bps margin improvement — a China/trade dynamic that ran *in Teledyne's favor* this quarter (management noted it was "nearly offset in dollar terms" by R&D + inventory-reserve increases, so the net-dollar benefit was modest, but it flattered the reported margin %). The structural irony still defines the page: **China is simultaneously Teledyne's demand driver (the adversary whose threat funds EO/IR + counter-UAS demand) and its supply risk (the controller of the rare-earth inputs its sensors need)** — but this quarter both the demand and the trade math broke Teledyne's way.

## Segment structure — where the defense/EO-IR sits

Four reportable segments (Q2 FY2026 weight, ≈ FY2025): **Digital Imaging (52%)** / **Instrumentation (23%)** / **Aerospace & Defense Electronics (17%)** / **Engineered Systems (7%)**. The **EO/IR chokepoint (Teledyne FLIR — thermal imagers, Black Hornet, Rogue 1, counter-UAS) sits inside Digital Imaging** — much of which is *commercial* (machine-vision cameras for semiconductor inspection, medical X-ray, DALSA/e2v imaging, MEMS). The A&D Electronics segment is the formal defense-electronics block. Honest read: the high-conviction EO/IR sensor chokepoint is a *sub-slice* of the 52% Digital Imaging segment, not the whole of it.

**AI-datacenter-adjacent slice (noted, not tier-framed).** Q2 added mild new color: **Teledyne LeCroy oscilloscopes** are seeing demand from "high bandwidth applications and power applications, including **people designing power supplies for data centers**," plus **terabit-Ethernet and PCI-Express protocol-analyzer** demand (Bobb) — alongside the standing **MEMS micromirrors for optical switching** touch. These are real but broad-cycle (no named hyperscaler; fail the Section 3.10 four-criterion test) → **no AI `*_tier`**; the slice stays a forward cross-thesis curiosity (the Teledyne analog of [[KTOS]]'s AI gas-turbine toehold, somewhat more substantive with the datacenter-protocol-test angle).

## Acquisitive compounder + capital allocation

Teledyne is a disciplined serial acquirer — ~75 acquisitions / ~$12.8B over 25 years (mostly cash-funded); ~90% of today's earnings come from businesses acquired over the past 25 years (Mehrabian). It is FCF-rich (>$1B/yr), now at **1.1× net leverage — the lowest since before the FLIR acquisition** — with $1.2B of untouched credit facility (and ~$4B of deployable capacity if it re-levered to the FLIR-deal level). It continues to favor **capex + R&D + M&A over buybacks** ("our demand is larger than our capacity in certain areas"), and is actively looking at both small and mid-range (>$1B) deals — but **won't overpay** ("we are not willing to pay some of the crazy prices… some people are outbidding us… by 30%–33%"). On the Framework D6 financial-quality screen, Teledyne is the **healthy end of the spectrum** (profitable, FCF-positive, low-leverage, no going-concern/dilution flags) — the structural opposite of the speculative Tier-3 micro-caps the screen was built for, and of [[KTOS]]'s capital-intensity profile.
