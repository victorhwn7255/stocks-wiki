# PLTR — Palantir Technologies Inc.

## Thesis role

Palantir is an AI-native data/analytics platform company (Gotham, Foundry, AIP) — application-layer software, a *consumer* of AI compute, not a supplier of any datacenter-supply-chain component. It is the third [[software-AI-moat-durability]] scorecard name ingested (after [[MSFT]] and [[NOW]]), chosen to test the *other end* of the billed-unit spectrum: the scorecard's cleanest **Durable / amplified** verdict (vs the two Contested-ish seat names), the **AI-native data-fusion** archetype, and a **deal/outcome-based** billed unit.

**Placement framing.** `layer: outside` + `outside` on all five per-domain tiers, following the demand-side / application-layer precedent set by [[MSFT]] and [[NOW]]. PLTR captures no supply-side chokepoint rent; its vault role is the research goal plus its cross-vault touchpoints (below). Per honest-verdict discipline (Section 2.1), its fit to the broader supply-chain thesis is weak by design. **Placement HELD at the Q2 2026 refresh** — a quarter of record growth strengthens the demand-side read but is not a supply-chain placement trigger.

**`frameworks.md` demand-side placement — RESOLVED (codified S189, 2026-07-19).** The application-layer placement gap is codified — **CLAUDE.md Section 3.21 + frameworks.md Framework 10.1** (PLTR = the **app-layer-consumer** sub-role, the third of the four flag instances). `_thesis.md` unchanged.

**`defense_tier: 2` added at the Q2 2026 refresh (S202).** The field was missing despite CLAUDE.md Section 3.2 naming PLTR among the dual-thesis pages that carry it and `frameworks-defense-drones.md` Framework D2 placing it at Tier 2 (mid-cap pure-play / specialist). Q2 2026 supplied the primary evidence the S114 baseline lacked — see Defense-thesis role.

**Research-goal verdict (validated at primary; see [[software-AI-moat-durability]]):** **Durable / amplified (execution-dependent)** — **HELD and strengthened** at Q2 2026. The "amplified" leg is now stronger still (93% revenue growth, Rule of 40 of 155, NDR 157%, US commercial +149%); the "execution-dependent" caveat is unchanged and real (government concentration, high-expectations valuation, heavy SBC, FDE-led GTM that is hard to scale).

**The monetization-gap claim (new at Q2, and the quarter's most thesis-relevant statement).** CTO Shyam Sankar: *"the market has created far more intelligence than it has converted into value"* — and *"a more powerful model does not solve this problem. The limiting factor is the rate of AIP deployment."* This is an app-layer primary source asserting that the constraint on AI value capture is **deployment**, not model capability — the claim [[AI-implementation-deployment-layer]] is built on, now stated at a vault primary. Sankar extended his prior framing from *"tokens are the new coal, AIP is the train"* to *"now our customers can build their own locomotives."*

## Moat — ontology + agentic execution, reframed as "sovereign AI"

The S114 read — moat = **ontology depth + agentic execution** rather than a classic usage-data flywheel — holds. Q2 2026 recast it under a single organizing frame, **"sovereign AI"** (the dominant term of the call, used ~18 times): the customer owns the weights, the data, the reasoning traces and the control plane, rather than renting capability from a frontier lab.

- **Model portability as product.** Management describes shipping the ability to swap models underneath deployed workflows — *"We're already doing that across the USG… We have a product that allows you to switch out models"* (Karp). Positioned explicitly against frontier-lab lock-in: *"if you are locked into a product, that's the nature of monopoly capitalism… then they can raise the prices and reduce the quality"* (Karp, rhetorical claim attributed).
- **Model commoditization.** *"The models are commodities, the American worker is not"* (Sankar). The company reports bringing NVIDIA's **Nemotron Ultra** into its stacks and finding five production tasks where the un-post-trained model beat frontier models within 24 hours — offered as evidence that public benchmarks are gameable and customer-specific benchmarks are what matter (*"benchmaxing has ended… the new era is benchmaking"*).
- **The frontier-lab bake-off.** Management describes a head-to-head evaluation at "a major Silicon Valley tech company" against "a frontier lab and its deployment team," which PLTR reports winning and converting to a $10M ACV contract — attributed as a management claim; the counterparty is unnamed and the account is one-sided.
- **Cost of the deployment model.** Adjusted gross margin was 86% in Q2, reduced by *"taking on cloud hosting for one of our Government customers"* — an application-layer name absorbing infrastructure cost directly. Management frames it as buying faster time-to-value and cost certainty; it is worth watching whether this becomes a structural mix shift (new Open question 6).

This remains the Durable/amplified leg — defensible, but tied to continued execution rather than a passive data-network effect.

## The NVIDIA partnership (counterparty-attribution-only)

New at Q2 2026: CEO Alex Karp stated *"we built a partnership with NVIDIA"*, describing models **fine-tuned by Palantir on an NVIDIA stack** — including in the classified space — that *"outperform frontier models, and you own the weights."* CTO Sankar's Nemotron Ultra result (above) is the technical datapoint alongside it.

**A1 three-mode annotation (Section 3.5): counterparty-attribution-only** — *named in the PLTR Q2 2026 earnings call; not reciprocally confirmed in [[NVDA]] primary sources.* No commercial instrument is disclosed — no equity, no supply agreement, no named program, no dollar figure. It is structurally a different kind of relationship from every instrument on [[nvidia-supply-chain-commitments]]: PLTR is neither an upstream component supplier nor a funded downstream capacity buyer, but an **application-layer / model-stack partner**. Recorded there as a new modality with NVDA-side bilateral verification pre-registered (Open question 5).

## Defense-thesis role (`defense_tier 2`)

PLTR is a dual-thesis page: an AI-datacenter demand-side app-layer consumer *and* a Defense & Drones name. `frameworks-defense-drones.md` places it at **Framework D2 Tier 2** (mid-cap pure-play / specialist) and, at Framework D5, as the thin US-listed exposure to the **autonomy & AI software** chokepoint — the layer the defense thesis rates "very high structurally, but mostly un-investable" because the best capability (Anduril, Shield AI) is private.

Q2 2026 primary evidence (call, Aug 3 2026):
- **Maven** — *"We had our first program in the Maven platform launched this past quarter, where a government program of record chose Maven as the platform that they will operate their program in"* (Sankar), taking advantage of "open data standards, ontology, developer tooling, peering, security." Maven also described as the developer/builder platform for the Joint Force with **over 25,000 builders**.
- **US government revenue $809M, +90% YoY** (+18% sequential), with momentum "across both defense and civil"; Government segment $990.0M (+79%; 10-Q).
- **Scale caveat, management's own:** *"our Department of War trailing 12-month revenue is still less than 25 basis points of the Pentagon's budget"* — i.e. the defense position is a fast-growing sliver of the addressable budget, not an incumbent share.

Per the D5 quality gradient, this is a **software/IP chokepoint**, not a geology or physics one — durable on integration depth and accreditation rather than on scarce atoms, and therefore ranked below the magnet and seeker chokepoints in structural durability. ## Growth & the execution-dependent caveats

The "amplified" metrics strengthened again: Q2 2026 revenue +93% YoY (a new high as a public company), US revenue +115% and now 81% of total, US commercial +149%, Rule of 40 of 155 (12th consecutive quarter of expansion), NDR 157%, adjusted FCF margin 63%, and the largest full-year guidance raise in company history.

The honest counterweight (the "execution-dependent" qualifier) is intact and in two places has moved the wrong way:

- **Government concentration ~51% (Q2 2026)** — down from ~53% at Q1 and ~42% for FY2025 as a full-year figure, and commercial is growing faster than government (+110% vs +79%). Directionally diversifying, but still roughly half the business, and US-government-budget/appropriations risk is a named 10-K risk factor.
- **Stock-based-comp dilution — widened.** The GAAP-vs-adjusted operating-margin gap moved from ~14 points (Q1) to ~15 points (Q2); SBC $265.2M (+66% YoY) against diluted shares of 2,568.7M (10-Q).
- **High-expectations execution** — management itself frames the bar as sustaining US-commercial-rate growth "for the next 18 months, which is a very high goal" (Karp). The business prices in sustained hyper-growth, leaving little margin for slips (described structurally; no valuation content per Section 2.1).
- **FDE-led GTM** — the forward-deployed-engineer motion that drives the ontology moat remains talent-intensive and structurally hard to scale; Karp flagged partnering as the intended answer (*"Behind the scenes now we're trying to find partners"*), which is itself an unproven scaling mechanism.
- **Strategic commercial contracts** (the SPAC-era cohort) are now immaterial — ~$400K in the quarter, 0.02% of revenue, expected below $500K per remaining quarter (call).

## Open questions

1. **AIP revenue disclosure.** Still not broken out at Q2 2026 (two consecutive quarters). Does PLTR ever disclose an AIP/AI revenue line at Tier 1, or does the AI signal stay visible only via growth rates and call-only TCV bookings?
2. **Government-vs-commercial mix — partially resolved, live remainder.** Government fell to ~51% of revenue at Q2 (from ~53% at Q1) with commercial growing faster (+110% vs +79%). The remaining question is whether the mix keeps diversifying toward commercial through FY2026, or whether the +90% US-government growth rate re-dominates.
3. **SBC dilution — widening, not narrowing.** The GAAP/adjusted operating-margin gap moved from ~14 to ~15 points across Q1→Q2. Does it stabilize as revenue scales, or persist as a structural drag on GAAP earnings quality?
4. **Execution vs expectations.** Can the business sustain the growth rate management has set as its own bar (US-commercial-rate growth for 18 months)?
5. **NVDA partnership scope — pre-registered bilateral verification.** What is the actual commercial substance of the "partnership with NVIDIA"? Watch for an NVDA-side disclosure at the next [[NVDA]] refresh, or a PLTR disclosure naming an instrument, program, or dollar figure. Currently counterparty-attribution-only.
6. **Cloud-hosting cost absorption.** Q2 adjusted gross margin (86%) absorbed cloud hosting for a Government customer. Is this a one-off contract structure, or the start of PLTR taking infrastructure cost onto its own P&L at scale — which would make an app-layer name a direct compute buyer and change its gross-margin profile?
7. **Defense-page placement.** PLTR now carries `defense_tier: 2` but is not yet a named participant on [[defense-drone-supply-chain]] (its autonomy / AI-software node). Deferred — best done alongside the parallel [[AMD]] / [[NVDA]] `defense_tier` backfill flagged at S202.
