# MSFT — Microsoft Corporation

## Thesis role

Microsoft is the vault's **first hyperscaler / demand-side company page**, ingested for two purposes: (1) as a **demand-side anchor** for calibrating supply-chain chokepoint exposure — MSFT is the source of the CAPEX flow the rest of the vault tracks downstream; and (2) as the **first primary-source test of the [[software-AI-moat-durability]] research goal** (which software moats AI amplifies vs. erodes). It is *not* ingested as a supply-chain value-capture participant.

**Honest verdict (FY2026 refresh — two-sided; the demand signal strengthened decisively, and *at expanding margins*).** The capex-payer demand read got materially stronger — and, crucially, the strength showed up **at expanding operating margins**, the direct rebuttal to the "capex is growing faster than revenue" worry. FY2026: revenue **$331.8B (+18%**, accelerating); **operating income $155.2B (+21%)** outpacing revenue, with **operating margins EXPANDING to 45%** despite AI-infra scaling; Microsoft Cloud **$214.4B (+27%)**; **Azure +41% for the year (+43% in Q4**, accelerating, with demand exceeding supply — visible in spot-market asset pricing); and **commercial remaining performance obligations exploded +84% to $678B** (MSFT FY2026 10-K + Q4/FY2026 call). Meanwhile the theme's contested software seat-leg is now being **actively hybridized toward consumption** ("seats → seats + consumption"). **But the honest counterweights hold:** (1) **FCF fell to $19.6B (Q4)** on the capex ramp — FY2026 cash capex was **$115.9B (+80%)** and CY2026 capex is guided ~$175–190B with FY2027 **growing** (MSFT stays FCF-positive, unlike [[ORCL]], but funding intensity is rising); (2) a **§2.1 useful-life accounting change** (extending datacenter/office building lives, effective FY2027) modestly flatters future depreciation and makes the headline capex figure non-comparable; (3) **GAAP net income (+31%) / EPS $17.95 (+32%) is flattered by ~$5.0B of net gains on MSFT's OpenAI investment** (+$0.67 EPS; FY2025 was a −$3.6B loss) — the **adjusted non-GAAP +22%** is the cleaner operating read. Per Section 2.1, the page leads with the strong demand signal and surfaces the funding + accounting nuances plainly.

**Placement framing (honest-verdict, Section 2.1).** A hyperscaler sits *above* Layer 1 in `frameworks.md` — it is the **demand source** (Framework 3 dependency map) and the **CAPEX deployer** (Framework 10 allocation starting point), not a position within the six supply-side value-capture layers. MSFT carries `layer: outside` and `outside` on all five per-domain tiers — it captures no supply-side chokepoint rent; it *funds* the chokepoints. Demand-side placement is **codified (CLAUDE.md §3.21 + frameworks.md Framework 10.1, S189)**: MSFT = the **capex-payer** sub-role, theme-anchored not framework-placed. A strong quarter reinforces the demand-source role; it does not create a supply-side position (honest-verdict-trigger discipline — no trigger fires). `_thesis.md` unchanged.

**Research-goal verdict (validated at primary; see [[software-AI-moat-durability]]).** MSFT's structure remains the cleanest illustration of the theme's **billed-unit discriminator**: its **consumption-metered** leg (Azure, +43%) is *amplified* by AI, while its **seat-metered** leg (M365 Copilot) is *contested* — and FY2026 shows the company itself **moving the billed unit** toward consumption (see AI monetization). The scorecard verdict — **Durable (infra/Azure) + Contested-but-adapting (Copilot seat monetization)** — holds and sharpens at primary.

## Segments & cloud

Three reportable segments: **Productivity & Business Processes** (M365 Commercial/Consumer, LinkedIn, Dynamics), **Intelligent Cloud** (Azure + server products), **More Personal Computing** (Windows, Gaming, Search/advertising, devices). Intelligent Cloud is the AI/Azure-bearing segment and the fastest-growing (Q4 +32%; Azure +43%). **Azure** grew **+41% for FY2026 (+43% Q4)**, accelerating, with "customer demand continues to exceed available capacity" (CFO); AI services are a growing within-Azure contributor. **Microsoft Cloud** reached **$214.4B (+27%)** at a **65% gross margin** (down YoY on AI-infra scaling, better than expected) — the Microsoft-Cloud gross-margin trajectory under inference load is the live instance of the margin-compression watch metric on [[software-AI-moat-durability]]. Notably, *company* operating margins **expanded** to 45% even as cloud gross margin compressed — operating leverage from the non-cloud base plus efficiency gains offset the AI-infra drag.

## AI monetization & the billed-unit lens

This section connects directly to the [[software-AI-moat-durability]] research goal — MSFT is the first scorecard name validated at primary, and FY2026 delivered the theme's most important update yet: **the company is moving the billed unit.**

- **★ "Seats → seats + consumption" hybridization (the contested-leg answer forming).** Management is "evolving our business model beyond per seat to per seat *plus consumption*": usage-based billing added to Copilot Cowork; **Dynamics 365 usage-based credit consumption +4x QoQ**; **GitHub Copilot moved to usage-based billing (June)** with consumption revenue accelerating. This is a positive adaptation *and* an implicit acknowledgment that pure per-seat monetization is under pressure — it moves the contested seat-leg toward the durable consumption model.
- **Consumption-billed (Durable / amplified):** Azure (+43%, accelerating) is metered by compute consumption; agent activity increases usage. The leg the supply-chain thesis ultimately serves.
- **Seat-billed (Contested-but-adapting):** M365 Copilot — **over 30M paid seats** (net adds >doubled QoQ, from >20M at Q3); EY deployed to 400,000 employees (largest win to date), NHS England to 505,000; GitHub Copilot 50M users (GitHub 225M); Copilot revenue +60% QoQ. Copilot penetration remains a low-to-mid single-digit share of the ~450M-seat M365 commercial base.
- **AI revenue is a call-only construct.** The Q3 "$37B AI-business ARR, +123%" was **call-only**; the FY2026 10-K carries **no discrete AI revenue line** (AI is embedded in Azure / M365 / GitHub; "annual recurring" does not appear in the 10-K). The Q4 prepared remarks did **not** restate a headline AI-ARR figure — the disclosure emphasis shifted to Copilot seat counts, Azure growth, and RPO. This is the MSFT analog of the disclosure-quality caveat the theme flags; see Source audit notes (Tier-1/Tier-2 framing gap).
- **Model-neutral platform (Nadella Q&A).** "Keep your harness separate from the model… any model is swappable"; Azure infrastructure "fungible" — delivers whatever model wins. MSFT co-develops its own **MAI** model family (MAI Thinking-1 reasoning model; 89% GPU-cost reduction in Dynamics voice, 84% in PowerPoint) alongside OpenAI + Anthropic — the "control-your-own-destiny" framing. Foundry has 100,000 customers (revenue >doubled YoY); Agent 365 has ~40M agents registered.

## CAPEX & demand-durability

MSFT is the vault's **first direct hyperscaler-CAPEX primary source** (see [[AI-demand-durability]] + the [[hyperscaler-capex]] payer-comparison tracker); Framework 10 (CAPEX flow) treats it as a top-of-diagram demand source.

- **Capex (the ramp).** FY2026 cash **additions to property & equipment $115.9B (+80%** from $64.6B; MSFT FY2026 10-K cash-flow statement); Q4 ~$41B including finance leases (~2/3 short-lived CPUs/GPUs correlating with revenue, ~1/3 long-lived datacenters/land; Q4 finance leases $5.6B; cash PP&E $35.8B). **Calendar-year 2026 capex** is guided **~$175B** on a lease-adjusted basis (~$190B unchanged-basis; see the useful-life note) and **FY2027 capex will GROW YoY** (Q1 FY2027 >$50B).
- **★ Margins EXPANDED — the disconnect rebuttal.** The investor worry that capex is outrunning revenue is directly answered this cycle: operating income (+21%) outpaced revenue (+18%) and operating margins rose to 45% *despite* the AI-infra ramp. CFO Amy Hood again addressed the capex-vs-revenue question, now with the margin-expansion result plus "demand continues to exceed available supply… you can see it in the spot-market pricing for assets" and efficiency gains across the CPU + GPU fleet.
- **★ FCF fell — funding intensity rising (the honest counterweight).** Operating cash flow was $55.4B (+30%, Q4) but **free cash flow fell to $19.6B** on the capex ramp. MSFT stays solidly **FCF-positive** and returned **>$43B to shareholders** in FY2026 — the opposite of [[ORCL]]'s negative-FCF funding-regime flip — but the intensity is climbing, and FY2027 capex grows again.
- **Backlog.** Commercial RPO **$678B (+84%)**; **+25% excluding OpenAI**; weighted-average duration 2.3 years; ~30% recognized in revenue over the next 12 months (+37% YoY). OpenAI is a material RPO component (its Azure commitments drive quarterly bookings/RPO volatility).

## Custom silicon

MSFT operates two in-house silicon lines (primary-source-confirmed; reciprocally confirm the [[MRVL]] Microsoft partnership on [[hyperscaler-custom-ASIC]]):

- **Maia 200** AI accelerator — **30% better performance-per-dollar** and **40% better performance-per-watt on MAI models** vs the latest fleet silicon; now supporting both OpenAI and MAI models; scaling (Q4/FY2026 call).
- **Cobalt 200** server CPU (Arm-based, alongside AWS Graviton / Google Axion) — in **25+ datacenters** by end of July, running Adobe, Arm, Elastic, OpenAI, Sprinklr, and TomTom workloads.

MSFT is also "among the first" cloud providers to deploy next-gen rack-scale AI infrastructure based on **AMD Helios** and **NVIDIA Vera Rubin** — the merchant + custom silicon mix.
