**[Biggest themes]** **Pasquariello:** Welcome to Breaks of the Game — Dominic, Josh, for our monthly. Same question to each: what's the single most interesting or important theme in markets right now? — **Wilson:** It's been unfair to funnel it into a single theme; there are ~two-and-a-half big ones with cross-currents. First, the AI theme — the investment boom and its beneficiaries — still the highest-level theme. Second, the re-ignition/re-escalation of oil and the Iran risks. Third, the Fed, really a sub-theme dominated by the other two. Different time horizons: the Fed is more local, oil is going to be with us a while, AI is the broader encompassing thing. If I condense it to one issue: shortages. We're discovering shortages matter — shortages for the AI supply chain, dominating power and semis and memory — and you create shortages in commodity markets too, in this case artificially, with a lot of friction. We're dealing with both.

**Pasquariello:** Josh? — **Schiffrin:** From 20,000 feet it's been a great year — S&P up close to double digits, some overseas markets up a lot, US economy strong, unemployment at the lows. But day-to-day it feels much worse; we're only ~2.5% off the highs in the S&P, but the lived experience feels much tougher — the under-the-surface volatility, popular trends reversing (momentum turning around), oil volatility, the prospect of monetary tightening. The collection of themes makes it feel much worse than the 20,000-feet view. Maybe a harbinger that the story is turning and these worries are adding up. — **Pasquariello:** Always the case, or particularly acute now? — **Schiffrin:** Particularly salient now — the heavy under-surface vol, oil's big rise then big fall the market tried to move on from, now reignited. Could be different this time without the fiscal boost coming in at the same time as the oil effect. Now maybe the Fed's in play, the rise in bond yields, the beginning of widening credit spreads — the market's getting more nervous around risks to the outlook, even though broadly it's been a very healthy year.

**[Next phase for AI]** **Pasquariello:** We're mid-reporting-period, earnings loom large next week. Dominic, you just published on AI — where are we headed in the next phase? — **Wilson:** We've already been moving into a new phase. Investment phase still firmly on track, confirmed this week; profitability still generally robust. But the value put into the system is large and has grown, and most of it in the last 6-9 months has gone to the infrastructure providers — which makes us more vulnerable to challenges, both to the overall size of the pie and to how it's shared out. Single-stock volatility has been rising persistently over 6-9 months, much less impact at the index level — a lot of this turn is underneath the surface. But we're getting more frequent bumps now — the semis challenge, challenges in other parts of the complex. There'll be renewed focus this earnings season on the hyperscalers' ability to monetize the spending they're doing; if they do, it could open things up further — there's been significant underperformance in that group and hope for a turn with clear earnings signals. On the other side, a lot of focus on the financing for the negative free cash flow coming through in some of these places, and the sustained financing needs. Although investment scaling up is good for the suppliers, you have to wonder whether worries about the sustainability of that investment profile spill over more generally. So the nature of the game now is still these tailwinds from the investment cycle and the profit side, but a lot more volatility around this theme — here to stay — and more two-sided risk than we've seen in a while.

**Pasquariello:** I've spent a lot of time with clients this past week — AI is still 75-80% of every conversation, for all the right reasons. To put a line under it: the momentum factor just went through a period where it realized the highest volatility in the past 45 years, with the exception of recessionary periods — a very high-velocity, high-volatility environment below the surface. That's not going to magically disappear. But the Fed variable has been reinserted into the short-term equation — the temperature is rising on US-Iran too, and public markets / new issue has been a more interesting topic. Mostly on the worry side, including AI-related worry. On AI: there's still no clear change in the willingness for the biggest spenders to keep spending. The question the market is asking is when can you most directly attach that to revenue — based on the earnings this week, that didn't happen. We'll have three more hyperscalers report next week, and it doesn't take much for them to send a positive signal — we saw that in Q1. That's what the market's still hunting for. But it's a more complex, more two-sided argument now. There was a real simplicity to it for a long time — just back the infrastructure plays, which worked remarkably well for the better part of 3.5 years. More complex the past couple of months.

**[Outlook for the Fed]** **Pasquariello:** The Fed meeting next week — a week ago it felt like a non-event, now it looks more interesting. — **Schiffrin:** Tough time for monetary policymakers. You got good news on correlation — a very low print in the US, well-behaved globally — and then re-escalation in oil prices puts a continued spotlight on inflation. You're now entering a period where every Fed meeting is live; to remain on hold they'll need the inflation picture coming in more benign. Interestingly, the backup in bond yields has really been a rise in real yields rather than breakeven inflation, even with the oil rise — I attribute that to the Fed talking tougher, Warsh coming in and talking tougher. To the extent the inflation picture remains challenged, I'd expect them at some point to hike; and if they don't, the balance shifts and inflation expectations widen. The market now prices hikes over the balance of this year into early next year, and it's that hawkishness keeping inflation expectations in check. A fluid situation — the picture looked a lot better following the softer print, but oil prices have rallied sharply the last few days. Last I looked, the probability of a hike next week was ~38%. — **Pasquariello:** Higher or lower in your view? — **Schiffrin:** Probably about right at this moment — a dynamic moment, oil up ~$6. They'll wait to take in everything up to the meeting. More broadly, we're moving into a period of a Fed that doesn't give forward guidance, and the environment is such that every meeting is live into year-end.

**[US-Iran re-escalation]** **Wilson:** On oil — until the last few days, broad markets have been remarkably relaxed about the reversal, partly because there's an ongoing feeling a resolution could occur at any point, and partly because what broad markets really care about is a major intensification of the military conflict or genuinely new supply channels. People got comfortable that oil could be higher or lower, and for most assets that matters relatively less. What's shifted uncomfortably is that those risks have escalated the last couple of days — attacks in the Red Sea opening up a new potential supply constraint that has to be resolved, and the prospect of more intense and broadening military action. So it's not surprising the market's paying more attention. The challenge is it's still hard to define the resolution path — we've seen quick moves to a resolution, and we've seen that resolution not hold, so lessons in both directions. There's real room for relief now that pricing has moved, but we're facing fresh risks that could be with us for a while. This last few days with the Houthi incursions and strikes has shifted the dynamics.

**[Non-AI convictions]** **Pasquariello:** Outside of AI, where do you have the most conviction — any market, any geography? — **Wilson:** A tricky patch — I'm thinking about things on the wrong end of the distribution; when volatility gets compressed across assets you're probably supposed to buy some of it. The thing I'm most interested in — and Josh referenced it — is the back-ends of yield curves. There's been a lot of focus on the Fed and the July FOMC, but the move in the 30-year yield through 5% was surprisingly quiet. Optionality has been much cheaper at the long end than the front. Real yields are clearly through the highs — a 30-year real yield close to 3% is an all-time high for that asset. I'm surprised, because that longer-dated rate is the one that matters most to markets and the economy. Ongoing risk there — not just US price risk but term-premium shocks from other parts of the world: the yen approaching 164 with pressure on the BOJ to pick up the pace, the UK going through a leadership transition. The back end has had too little focus; the upside tail has been underpriced — more interesting to me than the near-term Fed path. — **Schiffrin:** A couple things. Credit spreads feel somewhat asymmetric given the very tight starting point — I can see multiple paths to widening, whether increased supply, geopolitics, or monetary-policy tightening. And for the first time this year you can see the beginnings of maybe the dollar starting to do better — if the Fed tightens while the US economy is strong and oil prices are higher, that's a cocktail for the dollar. And we're entering a lower-liquidity August; maybe not a bad idea to keep things simpler and to the higher-conviction ideas. There are a lot of cross-currents, and even if the broad long-term trend is supported by the AI build-out, it feels like a choppy moment.
